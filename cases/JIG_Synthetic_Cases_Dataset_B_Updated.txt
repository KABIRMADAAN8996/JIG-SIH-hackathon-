CASE 6 — CONCLUDED/DISPOSED CASE (BACKEND REGRESSION TEST)
============================================================

Purpose:
Confirms that Rule 3 does not misfire on a case that concluded properly.

This case exists only as a backend test fixture. It is NOT part of the live demo sequence.

Expected result:
Rule 1: NOT TRIGGERED
Rule 2: NOT TRIGGERED
Rule 3: NOT TRIGGERED
Rule 4: NOT TRIGGERED
Overall: CLEAN

Important check:
A hearing with no next hearing date must NOT automatically mean "stalled."
The outcome must be checked. A properly "disposed" or "concluded" case must not trigger Rule 3.


Narrative:

State v. Deepak Rao

FIR No. FIR-606/2026 was filed at West Zone Police Station on 3 March 2026 following a complaint regarding an alleged offence. The complainant was Suresh Iyer and the accused was Deepak Rao. The FIR recorded the relevant legal sections applicable to the complaint.

The matter was registered as Case No. CR-606/26 before the District Court on 12 March 2026.

The chargesheet was filed on 10 April 2026. The chargesheet deadline recorded for this case was 10 June 2026.

The first hearing, Hearing H-601, was held on 2 May 2026 and was adjourned.

The latest hearing, Hearing H-602, was held on 20 June 2026. Its outcome was recorded as "disposed." No next hearing date was recorded because none was needed — the case had concluded.

Order O-601 was issued on 20 June 2026 recording the final disposal of the case.

As of 19 August 2026, 60 days have passed since the latest hearing, which is well past the 30-day threshold used by Rule 3. However, because the latest hearing outcome is "disposed" and not a continuing state such as "adjourned" or "ongoing," Rule 3 must NOT trigger.


Why Case 6 matters:

Without this regression case, the original five cases cannot verify whether the outcome check in Rule 3 is actually implemented. Cases 4 and 5 both use "adjourned" outcomes, so an incorrect Rule 3 implementation that ignored outcome entirely and checked only "no next hearing date + more than 30 days" could still incorrectly pass all five original tests.

Case 6 specifically catches that bug.

RULE REMINDERS
==============

Rule 1 — Case Registration Delay:
FIR exists, no Case has been registered, and current date - filed_date > 15 days.

Rule 2 — Hearing Scheduling Bottleneck:
Case is registered, no Hearing exists, and current_date - registration_date > 60 days.

Rule 3 — Stalled Proceedings:
The latest Hearing has a continuing outcome such as "adjourned" or "ongoing", next_hearing_date is NULL, and current_date - hearing_date > 30 days.
The outcome check is mandatory.
An outcome such as "disposed" or "concluded" must prevent the rule from triggering.

Rule 4 — Chargesheet Deadline Exceeded:
chargesheet_filed = false and current_date > chargesheet_deadline.
The deadline is the per-case value supplied by the dataset. There is no universal hardcoded 90-day rule.
